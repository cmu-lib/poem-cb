### Framing Prompting

Before we discuss the concept of prompting, we need to explain some AI-related terms that are relevant to understanding prompting.

- Large Language Model (LLM): Pre-trained on vast amounts of data from the Internet and designed for natural language processing tasks such as translation, text summarization, text generation, etc. One example of an LLM is GPT-3. 
- Large Multimodal Model (LMM): LMMs are distinct from LLMs due to their different modality focuses, as LLMs are designed for text-based tasks, while LMMs are capable of processing various modalities such as text, images, audio and videos. To be specific, LMMs can receive input in various modalities like text, images, audio, and video, and can also produce output in different modalities.[^Chen2024] Some examples of LMMs are GPT-5, DALL-E, and Gemini 2.0. 
- Chatbot: A computer program designed to simulate conversation with human users, especially over the internet.[^OxfordUniversityPress2023]
- AI Chatbot: A chatbot powered by LLMs or LMMs.[^Chen2024] Some examples of this are ChatGPT, Copilot, Gemini, etc. 

[^Chen2024]: Yinlin Chen, "Efficient Prompt Engineering for Librarians," Association of College & Research Libraries, May–June 2024, Online Course, [acrl.ala.org](https://acrl.ala.org/acrlinsider/efficient-prompt-engineering-for-librarians-online-course/).
[^OxfordUniversityPress2023]: Oxford English Dictionary, “chatbot (n.),” July 2023, [doi.org/10.1093/OED/1140056711](https://doi.org/10.1093/OED/1140056711).

In the context of AI, prompting describes any interaction or communication with AI models.[^Becker2024] A prompt is an input query you enter in an AI chatbot (built upon AI models) that sets the direction of the AI model’s response.[^Chen2024] Effective prompting, also called prompt engineering, is about crafting specific and effective prompts to guide AI models towards desired outputs.[^Bell2024]

[^Becker2024]: Kimberly P. Becker, Jessica L. Parker, and Desi Richter, "Framework for the Future: Building AI Literacy in Higher Education," White Paper, Moxie (June 2024), [moxielearn.ai](https://web.archive.org/web/20250804191745/https://moxielearn.ai/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Ai-literacies-white-paper.docx.pdf)
[^Bell2024]: Tyler Bell, "Generative artificial intelligence Tools: GhatGPT and Beyond," University of Iowa College of Engineering, ECE:5995:0004, Fall 2024, Course, [uiowa.edu](https://myui.uiowa.edu/my-ui/courses/details.page?ci=148347&id=1022726).

Educators and students need to understand not only how AI works, its capabilities and limitations, but also how to prompt because many industries now embrace AI for efficiency and innovation. Beckeralso observes the urgency of fostering AI literacy in educational settings to prevent misconceptions about AI tools; some students, for example, may use it inappropriately without guidance, while their teachers may distrust AI and prohibit its use altogether. As prompting is the starting point of interaction with AI, it’s timely for everyone in educational settings, especially college students, to have a basic understanding of effective prompting.[^Becker2024]

### Context & Scholarship and Critical Considerations

The concept of prompting (i.e., prompt engineering, effective prompting) is relatively new. When ChatGPT was publicly released in November 2022, prompt engineering was predicted to be a "hot job,”[^Kelly2024] and an MIT professor described it as the single most important skill for employees.[^Huddleson2023] Since that time, the possibilities of what AI can do for us through prompting have only grown. For instance, AI Image generators like Midjourney can generate images from prompts, but ChatGPT and Copilot now have the capability to do this as well. Although text-to-speech technology already exists that bolsters accessibility for individuals with disabilities, AI voice technology significantly enhances their user experience. ElevenLabs provides AI voice models for creating realistic voices and even cloning a real person’s voice.[^ElevenLabs] YouTube’s automatic dubbing feature can generate translated audio in different languages to make content more accessible to viewers around the world.[^Youtube] Furthermore, you may remember a video of two pirate ships battling inside a cup of coffee. That fascinating video was generated, using prompts, by OpenAI’s Sora.

[^Kelly2024]: Jack Kelly, "The Hot, New High-Paying Career Is An AI Prompt Engineer," *Forbes*, March 06, 2024,[forbes.com](https://www.forbes.com/sites/jackkelly/2024/03/06/the-hot-new-high-paying-career-is-an-ai-prompt-engineer/).

However, Acar has predicted that the prominence of prompt engineering may be fading, because future generations of AI will understand our intentions without deliberate prompts.[^Acar2023] He also points out that students should focus on problem formulation rather than prompt engineering. In a similar vein, Loukides argues the importance of prompting has been overrated. He recommends that students should first instruct AI to solve a problem step by step.[^Loukides2023] Indeed, prompt engineering has become increasingly obsolete due to the rapid advancement of AI.[^Bousquette2025]

[^Acar2023]: Oguz A. Acar, "AI Prompt Engineering Isn’t the Future," *Harvard Business Review*, June 6, 2023, [hbr.org](https://hbr.org/2023/06/ai-prompt-engineering-isnt-the-future). 
[^Loukides2023]: Mike Loukides, "Prompting Isn’t The Most Important Skill," *Radar*, October 17, 2023, [oreilly.com](https://www.oreilly.com/radar/prompting-isnt-the-most-important-skill/).
[^Bousquette2025]: Isabelle Bousquette, "The Hottest AI Job of 2023 Is Already Obsolete," *Wall Street Journal*, April 25, 2025, [wsj.com](https://www.wsj.com/articles/the-hottest-ai-job-of-2023-is-already-obsolete-1961b054).

Despite the importance of prompting rapidly changing in the past years, we encourage educators and students to continue interacting with AI, as AI tools are increasingly being integrated into educational settings. While AI presents promising opportunities for improving learning outcomes in current literature, Han et. al. argue that the impact of AI in educational settings varies by educational level, subject, and time duration. For example, AI may yield greater impact in primary and secondary education than in higher education. It has been shown that AI interventions are more effective in structured disciplines, such as natural sciences, and under conditions of short-term exposure. Therefore, we encourage educators to implement discipline-specific strategies and design task-based activities around optimal time duration when teaching with AI.[^Han2025]

[^Han2025]: Xiaoli Han, Hongchao Peng, and Mingzhuo Liu, "The impact of GenAI on learning outcomes: A systematic review and meta-analysis of experimental studies," *Educational Research Review* 48 (2025) [doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2025.100714](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2025.100714).

Additionally, we suggest that educators should encourage students to spend a couple of hours learning prompting techniques. However, students should be aware that domain knowledge and problem-solving skills are essential for effectively leveraging AI. Consider one example of using AI to generate a webpage. Students should understand the coding language (e.g., HTML, CSS, and JavaScript) they need, possess debugging skills, and know the right jargon to put in the prompts. 

While students and educators alike can benefit from using AI in educational settings, they should also be aware of potential issues involved in the AI itself. MIT’s AI resource hub warns that AI can produce biased, fabricated, and unethical content due to training data sources, limitations of AI models, and inherent challenges in AI design.[^MIT]

[^MIT]: "When AI Gets It Wrong: Addressing AI Hallucinations and Bias," MIT Sloan Teaching & Learning Technologies, [AI Resource Hub](https://mitsloanedtech.mit.edu/ai/basics/addressing-ai-hallucinations-and-bias/).

#### Bias

AI is likely to perpetuate gender and racial bias. For example, in the initial preview stage of DALL-E 2 (A I image generator), Mishkin found that DALL-E 2 tends to overrepresent people who are white-passing and western concepts.[^Mishkin2022] Cheong et. al. also found problematic images produced by DALL-E Mini (DALL-E 3’s open-source derivative). For example, in generating images of some occupations, such as farmer, painter, prison officer, and software engineer, DALL-E Mini predominantly generated pictures of white men, and in generating occupations like hairdresser, receptionist, and dietitian, DALL-E Mini produced images of exclusively women.[^Cheong2024]

#### Hallucination

AI has the potential to produce factual errors, fabricated content, and irrelevant, un-asked for outputs. One notable example of AI producing factual errors occurred when GPT-4 incorrectly stated that 3,821 is not a prime number.[^Franschlader2025] AI can generate citations that do not exist. In the legal case of Mata v. Avianca, the plaintiff’s lawyer used ChatGPT to generate a legal motion with numerous internal citations and quotes that did not exist.[^Weiser2023] Furthermore, consider my own experience of using NotebookLM, which is built with Gemini. Gemini generated a podcast, based on lecture slides I provided, about research data storage and security. However, the podcast included content about internet security that was not present in my slides.

#### Jailbreaking

Although AI models set rules to enforce safety and moderation to avoid providing unethical, violent, sexual and illegal content, the rules can be bypassed through careful contextual framing.[^Tyler2024] For example, Bing Chat (Microsoft’s AI chatbot) refused to solve the CAPTCHA (a security puzzle featuring distorted letters or numbers, designed to be difficult for computers to recognize but easy for human to decipher) when a user showed it a simple image of the CAPTCHA. However, the user broke the bot’s rules and made the bot solve the puzzle by shifting the context: they showed the bot an image of a locket necklace with the CAPTCHA on it, as well as a prompt about a grandmother and the memory tied to the necklace.[^Edwards2023]
