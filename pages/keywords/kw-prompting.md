### Framing Prompting

Before we discuss the concept of prompting, we need to explain some AI-related terms that are relevant to understanding prompting.

- Large Language Model (LLM): Pre-trained on vast amounts of data from the Internet and designed for natural language processing tasks such as translation, text summarization, text generation, etc. One example of an LLM is GPT-3. 
- Large Multimodal Model (LMM): LMMs are distinct from LLMs due to their different modality focuses, as LLMs are designed for text-based tasks, while LMMs are capable of processing various modalities such as text, images, audio and videos. To be specific, LMMs can receive input in various modalities like text, images, audio, and video, and can also produce output in different modalities [(Chen, 2024)](https://acrl.ala.org/acrlinsider/). Some examples of LMMs are GPT-5, DALL-E, and Gemini 2.0. 
- Chatbot: A computer program designed to simulate conversation with human users, especially over the internet. [Oxford University Press, n.d., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1093/OED/2981785869)
- AI Chatbot: A chatbot powered by LLMs or LMMs [(Chen, 2024)](https://acrl.ala.org/acrlinsider/). Some examples of this are ChatGPT, Copilot, Gemini, etc. 

In the context of AI, prompting describes any interaction or communication with AI models [(Becker, 2024)](https://web.archive.org/web/20250804191745/https://moxielearn.ai/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Ai-literacies-white-paper.docx.pdf). A prompt is an input query you enter in an AI chatbot (built upon AI models) that sets the direction of the AI model’s response [(Chen, 2024)](https://acrl.ala.org/acrlinsider/). Effective prompting, also called prompt engineering, is about crafting specific and effective prompts to guide AI models towards desired outputs [(Bell, 2024)](https://myui.uiowa.edu/my-ui/courses/details.page?ci=148347&id=1022726). 

Educators and students need to understand not only how AI works, its capabilities and limitations, but also how to prompt [(Becker, 2024)](https://web.archive.org/web/20250804191745/https://moxielearn.ai/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Ai-literacies-white-paper.docx.pdf), because many industries now embrace AI for efficiency and innovation. [Becker (2024)](https://web.archive.org/web/20250804191745/https://moxielearn.ai/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Ai-literacies-white-paper.docx.pdf) also emphasizes the urgency of fostering AI literacy in educational settings to prevent misconceptions about AI tools. For example, some students may use it inappropriately without guidance, while their teachers may distrust AI and prohibit its use altogether. As prompting is the starting point of interaction with AI, it’s imperative for everyone in educational settings, especially college students, to have a basic understanding of effective prompting. 

### Context & Scholarship and Critical Considerations

The concept of prompting (i.e., prompt engineering, effective prompting) is relatively new. When ChatGPT was publicly released in November 2022, prompt engineering was predicted to be a hot “job” [(Kelly, 2024)](https://www.forbes.com/sites/jackkelly/2024/03/06/the-hot-new-high-paying-career-is-an-ai-prompt-engineer/), and an MIT professor described it as the single most important skill for employees [(CNBC, 2023)](https://www.cnbc.com/2023/09/22/tech-expert-top-ai-skill-to-know-learn-the-basics-in-two-hours.html). Since that time, the possibilities of what AI can do for us through prompting have only multiplied. For instance,  ChatGPT and Copilot can now generate images from written prompts, a capability previously limited to specialized LMMs like Midjourney. Although text-to-speech technology that bolsters accessibility for individuals with disabilities already exists, AI voice technology significantly enhances their user experience. ElevenLabs provides AI voice models for creating realistic voices and even cloning a real person’s voice [(ElevenLabs)](https://elevenlabs.io/). YouTube’s automatic dubbing feature can generate translated audio in different languages to make content more accessible to viewers around the world [(YouTube Help)](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15569972?hl=en). Furthermore, you may remember a video of two pirate ships battling inside a cup of coffee. That fascinating video was generated, using prompts, by OpenAI’s Sora. 

However, [Acar (2023)](https://hbr.org/2023/06/ai-prompt-engineering-isnt-the-future) has predicted that the prominence of prompt engineering may be fading, because future generations of AI will understand our intentions without deliberate prompts. He also points out that students should focus on problem formulation rather than prompt engineering. In a similar vein, [Loukides (2023)](https://www.oreilly.com/radar/prompting-isnt-the-most-important-skill/) argues the importance of prompting has been overrated. He recommends that students should first instruct AI to solve a problem step by step. Indeed, [Bousquette (2025)](https://www.wsj.com/articles/the-hottest-ai-job-of-2023-is-already-obsolete-1961b054) asserts that prompt engineering has become increasingly obsolete due to the rapid advancement of AI. 

Despite the rapid advances in prompting, we encourage educators and students to continue interacting with AI as AI tools are integrated into educational settings. While AI presents promising opportunities for improving learning outcomes in current literature, [Han et. al. (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2025.100714) argues that the impact of AI in educational settings varies by educational level, subject, and time duration. For example, AI may yield greater impact in primary and secondary education than in higher education. It has been shown that AI interventions are more effective in structured disciplines, such as natural sciences, and under conditions of short-term exposure. Therefore, we encourage educators to implement discipline-specific strategies and design task-based activities around optimal time duration when teaching with AI [(Han, 2025)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2025.100714). 

Additionally, we suggest that educators should encourage students to spend a couple of hours learning prompting techniques. However, students should be aware that domain knowledge and problem-solving skills are essential for effectively leveraging AI. Consider one example of using AI to generate a webpage. Students should understand the coding language (e.g., HTML, CSS, and JavaScript) they need, possess debugging skills, and know the right jargon to put in the prompts. 

While students and educators alike can benefit from using AI in educational settings, they should also be aware of potential issues involved in the AI itself. MIT’s AI resource hub warns that AI can produce biased, fabricated, and unethical content due to training data sources, limitations of AI models, and inherent challenges in AI design [(MIT)](https://mitsloanedtech.mit.edu/ai/basics/addressing-ai-hallucinations-and-bias/). 

#### Bias

AI models are trained on flawed data or overrepresented perspectives scraped from the internet, thus they generate content that perpetuates stereotypes and biased perspectives. For example, in the initial preview stage of DALL-E 2 (A I image generator), [Mishkin (2022)](https://github.com/openai/dalle-2-preview/blob/main/system-card.md#bias-and-representation) found that DALL-E 2 tends to overrepresent people who are white-passing and western concepts. [Cheong et. al. (2024)](https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3649883) also found problematic images produced by DALL-E Mini (DALL-E 3’s open-source derivative). For example, in generating images of some occupations, such as farmer, painter, prison officer, and software engineer, DALL-E Mini predominantly generated pictures of white men, and in generating occupations like hairdresser, receptionist, and dietitian, DALL-E Mini produced images of exclusively women. 

#### Hallucination

AI has the potential to produce factual errors, fabricated content, and irrelevant, un-asked for outputs. One notable example of AI producing factual errors occurred when GPT-4 incorrectly stated that 3,821 is not a prime number [(Franschlader, 2025)](https://www.datacamp.com/blog/ai-hallucination). AI can generate citations that do not exist. In the legal case of Mata v. Avianca, the plaintiff’s lawyer used ChatGPT to generate a legal motion with numerous internal citations and quotes that did not exist [(Weiser, 2023)](https://www.nytimes.com/2023/05/27/nyregion/avianca-airline-lawsuit-chatgpt.html). Furthermore, consider my own experience of using NotebookLM, which is built with Gemini. I asked Gemini to generate a podcast, based on lecture slides I provided, about research data storage and security. However, the podcast included content about internet security that was not present in my slides. Some strategies to help mitigate hallucinations include critically evaluating outputs with human judgement [(MIT Management STS Teaching and Learning Technologies)](https://mitsloanedtech.mit.edu/ai/basics/addressing-ai-hallucinations-and-bias/) and setting clear expectations in the prompts such as “any facts described must be supported by a scholarly source. Include working links to the parent article.”   

#### Jailbreaking

Although AI models set rules to enforce safety and moderation to avoid providing unethical, violent, sexual and illegal content, the rules can be bypassed through careful contextual framing [(Tyler, 2024)](https://myui.uiowa.edu/my-ui/courses/details.page?ci=148347&id=1022726). For example, Bing Chat (Microsoft’s AI chatbot) refused to solve the CAPTCHA (a security puzzle featuring distorted letters or numbers, designed to be difficult for computers to recognize but easy for humans to decipher) when a user showed it a simple image of the CAPTCHA. However, the user broke the bot’s rules and made the bot solve the puzzle by shifting the context: they showed the bot an image of a locket necklace with the CAPTCHA on it, as well as a prompt about a grandmother and the memory tied to the necklace [(Edwards, 2023)](https://arstechnica.com/information-technology/2023/10/sob-story-about-dead-grandma-tricks-microsoft-ai-into-solving-captcha/). 

### Closing Reflection

These teaching and learning objects are selected based on the students’ preferences and time constraints, ensuring a flexible and engaging learning experience. Prompt Perfect: Elevating Your AI Experience is ideal for beginners who prefer attending a hands-on workshop. AI Challenge: Step 1 to 9 is well-suited for self-guided students seeking to build prompting skills through challenge-based activities. Educators can also incorporate these activities into workshops or classroom sessions to enhance hands-on learning. AI in Education not only supports self-guided students with in-depth content, but also serves as a reference book for educators. AI Prompt Cookbook provides ready-to-use “recipes” that make AI more accessible for educators and students. While students should have a basic understanding of effective prompting, they should not rely solely on prompting techniques without developing problem-solving skills, communication skills, critical thinking skills, and domain knowledge.
